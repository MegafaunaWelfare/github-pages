High Court issues notice on our petition over the Chandigarh SPCA

The Punjab and Haryana High Court has issued notice on a public interest litigation filed by Megafauna Welfare Foundation, through advocate Harpreet Pal Singh Bunger, that challenges the UT administration's takeover of the Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (SPCA) in Chandigarh.

The petition argues that the SPCA, registered in 1986 under the Societies Registration Act, is an autonomous charity, and that the takeover goes against the rules that govern SPCAs. It asks the court to set aside the orders that put officials in charge, and for a detailed audit, free and fair elections to the society's governing body, and the society's assets to be handed over to the elected body.

AdvocacyJuly 2026

Speaking up for the campus dogs of PEC

Before the Prime Minister's visit on 17 July, community dogs were picked up from the Punjab Engineering College (PEC) campus and taken to the Animal Birth Control centre at Raipur Kalan. More than a week later, many of them were still there.

Under the Animal Birth Control Rules, 2023, sterilised community dogs are to be returned to the area they were picked up from. Our co-founder Udit Bhatia told The Tribune that the Supreme Court had nowhere barred visits or photography at ABC centres. After pressure from students and animal welfare activists, PEC agreed to take the remaining dogs back.

Why dog sterilisation isn't working: it's the implementation

At a panel discussion on the Animal Birth Control Rules at the Press Club of India, organised by Compassionate Citizens of India, our director Udit Bhatia said that sterilisation figures are often quoted without transparent data, and that tenders and monitoring committees often aren't working.

"Where ABC rules have been implemented properly, complaint rates and dog bite cases have sharply fallen," he said, as reported by ThePrint.
