Animal in need?

Found an injured or sick animal? Here's what to do.

A few calm minutes can save a life. Follow these steps, then report it so our team can help.

Report it in our app Cruelty happening now? Call 112

Step by step

Five things to do first.

Then send the rescue team a report with the location and photos. Our free app puts it together for you in under a minute.

Report it in our app

  1. Keep yourself and the animal safe

    Approach slowly and quietly. A frightened or hurt animal may bite. If it is near traffic, alert drivers and keep a safe distance.

  2. Note the exact location

    A landmark, sector number and a photo or short video help the rescue team find the animal quickly and judge what it needs.

  3. Contact us or a nearby vet

    Share the location and photos. Tell us whether the animal can move, is bleeding, or seems to be in pain.

  4. Offer water and shade, never medicine

    Don't give human medicines. Many common painkillers are poisonous to animals. Water and shade are safe ways to help.

  5. Stay nearby if you can

    Keep an eye on the animal until help arrives so it doesn't wander off or get lost.

First aid

First aid for common emergencies.

Simple, safe things anyone can do while help is on the way. This isn't a substitute for a vet.

Hit by a vehicle

Do

  • Make sure you are safe from traffic first. Ask someone to slow vehicles down.
  • If the animal has to be moved off the road, slide a blanket, sack or board under its whole body and lift it level, keeping its back straight.
  • Keep it warm, quiet and out of the sun while you wait.

Don't

  • Don't lift it by the legs, neck or scruff.
  • Don't force food or water on it, and never give human medicine.
Injured or bleeding

Do

  • If it is safe to get close, press a clean cloth firmly on the wound for a few minutes to slow the bleeding.
  • A towel laid loosely over the head calms many frightened animals.
  • Note where the wound is and roughly how big, so the team brings the right supplies.

Don't

  • Don't put kerosene, phenyl or other chemicals on a wound.
  • Don't give human painkillers. Paracetamol and ibuprofen can poison dogs and cats.
Wound with maggots

Do

  • Get help the same day. Maggot wounds spread fast and can kill an animal.
  • Keep flies off by loosely covering the wound with clean gauze or cloth.
  • Note exactly where the animal rests. Animals in pain often hide.

Don't

  • Don't pour kerosene, phenyl, acid or other chemicals into the wound. They burn the flesh and can poison the animal.
  • Don't try to dig the maggots out yourself.
Sick or very weak

Do

  • Offer clean water in a bowl, and help it into shade if you can do so safely.
  • Note what you see: vomiting, diarrhoea, discharge from the eyes or nose, shivering, or not eating.
  • Keep puppies and kittens warm and dry.

Don't

  • Don't give human medicines.
  • Don't force-feed.
Suspected poisoning

Do

  • Get the animal to a vet as fast as you can. Minutes matter.
  • Put any leftover bait, packet or vomit in a bag. It helps the vet, and it is evidence.
  • Note who was around and what you saw. Poisoning an animal is a crime under section 325 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita.

Don't

  • Don't make the animal vomit unless a vet tells you to.
  • Don't give milk, oil or salt water.
Heatstroke

Watch for heavy panting, drooling, bright red gums, wobbling or collapse.

Do

  • Move the animal into shade straight away.
  • Wet its body with cool water, especially the belly and paws, and let it drink small amounts.
  • Fan it if you can.

Don't

  • Don't use ice or ice-cold water.
  • Don't leave a wet towel lying on it. It traps the heat.
Trapped or tangled

Do

  • Keep people back and talk softly. A trapped animal panics easily.
  • For a bird caught in kite string, cover its head with a cloth and cut the string close to its body with scissors.
  • For an animal in a drain, pit or pipe, tell the team how deep it is and what is in the way.

Don't

  • Don't pull string or wire that is wrapped around a leg or wing. It cuts deeper.
  • Don't climb into deep drains or wells yourself.

Nylon and other synthetic kite string ('Chinese manjha') has been banned across India by the National Green Tribunal since 2017.

Puppies or kittens in danger

Do

  • Watch from a distance for a couple of hours first. The mother is often nearby looking for food.
  • If they are in immediate danger from traffic, rain or a drain, move them a short way to a safe, dry spot close by.
  • Keep tiny ones warm.

Don't

  • Don't give cow's milk. It upsets their stomachs.
  • Don't take them far from where you found them, or the mother may not find them.
Cruelty or abuse

Do

  • If someone is hurting an animal right now and it isn't safe to step in, call the police on 112.
  • From a safe distance, take photos or video, and note the date, time, place and any vehicle numbers.
  • Write down the names and numbers of people who saw it and are willing to back you up.

Don't

  • Don't confront anyone if it puts you at risk.
  • Don't edit or delete the original photos and videos. They may be needed as evidence.

Cruelty to animals is an offence under the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960. Killing, poisoning or maiming an animal is punishable with up to five years in prison under section 325 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023.

Report cruelty

Seen an animal being hurt? Here's how to make it count.

Good evidence is what turns a complaint into a case. Record what you safely can, then tell us. Our Legal Aid Project can help you take it to the police.

Report a case Know the law

  1. Your safety comes first

    Don't confront anyone. If a person or animal is in danger right now, call the police on 112.

  2. Photograph and film before anything is moved

    The animal and its injuries, close up and from further away, the place, and any food, packets, weapons, vehicles or number plates. Keep your camera's date and location settings on.

  3. Keep the originals as they are

    Don't edit, crop or filter your photos and videos, and don't delete them. They may be needed as evidence.

  4. Note the witnesses

    Names and phone numbers of people who saw what happened and are willing to back you up. Ask nearby shops and homes about CCTV straight away, as footage is often recorded over within days.

  5. Get the animal to a vet

    Ask for a written injury report, or a post-mortem if the animal has died. It's often the strongest evidence.

  6. Tell us, and go to the police

    Send us what you have. You can give a written complaint at any police station, and we can help if it isn't registered.

For feeders

Looking after community animals.

Good habits that keep animals healthy and neighbours on side.

Feed at the same spot and time

Choose a spot your neighbours agree on, away from play areas, gates and entrances. Use bowls or leaves rather than plastic, and clean up afterwards. Animals that know your routine are also easier to catch for sterilisation and vaccination.

Look for the ear notch

Under the Animal Birth Control Rules, community dogs are sterilised, vaccinated and returned to their area. A small V-shaped notch in one ear usually means a dog has already been sterilised.

Keep up with rabies boosters

Vets usually advise a yearly anti-rabies booster for community dogs. The WHO says vaccinating at least 70% of the dogs in an area stops rabies spreading.

If you are stopped from feeding

Stay calm and don't argue. Ask your RWA or the municipal corporation where the designated feeding spots are, and get in touch with us. Our Legal Aid Project can help.
